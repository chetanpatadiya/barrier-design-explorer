Barrier Design Explorer

Explore ideal steady-state oxygen and moisture transmission through multilayer packaging films. Use your own permeability values; compare layer thickness and relative barrier contributions.

ILLUSTRATIVE DEMO • Synthetic coefficients
Development-stage engineering screening tool — not validated for design release. Results use ideal planar layers with constant permeability. They do not model moisture-dependent EVOH behavior, temperature-dependent material coefficients, layer defects, thermoforming, package geometry, seal integrity, or sterile barrier performance. Independent test data are required for actual packaging decisions.
DEMO DATA ARE SYNTHETIC. Preloaded coefficients are invented solely to demonstrate the mathematical relationships. They are not literature values, supplier specifications, or performance predictions. Select “Start with my data” before using material-specific inputs.

1. Environment & driving force

Isothermal · common conditions for all layers

Temperature is used only to convert RH to water-vapor partial pressure (Buck 1996 correlation). Polymer permeability coefficients remain constant when temperature or RH changes. Enter coefficients measured under appropriate conditions.

2. Layer architecture (3 layers)

Supply pressure-normalized permeability coefficients, not an OTR/WVTR measured for a complete film. Do not enter zero for an ideal foil or impermeable coating; this model does not support defect-controlled barriers. Every layer's permeability is required for its corresponding transmission calculation.

    Method, equations & source notes

    Model implemented in V0.1

    Rk,total = Σ [ Li / Pk,i ]
    Jk = Δpk / Rk,total

    For oxygen, Δp is entered in atmospheres. For water vapor, Δp = psat(T)·(RHoutside − RHinside)/100 in kPa. Reported WVTR is the magnitude; direction is reported separately. Resistance share = Ri/ΣRi.

    Boundary of applicability

    Ideal, homogeneous, defect-free planar layers; one steady-state flux; no interface resistance, no humidity-dependent P, no transient water sorption or oxygen–water coupling. Layer order does not affect this model's result.

    Public supporting references

    2. Morris (2013) — multilayer packaging resistance model in a package-design context.
